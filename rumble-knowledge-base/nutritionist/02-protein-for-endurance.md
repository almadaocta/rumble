# Protein for Endurance Athletes

## Overview

Protein is essential for muscle repair, recovery, and adaptation to endurance training. Endurance athletes have higher protein needs than the general population, though not as high as strength/power athletes. Distribution across the day matters as much as total daily intake.

## Daily Protein Targets

Based on ISSN Position Stand (Jäger et al., 2017) and Phillips & Van Loon (2011):

### Endurance Athletes
- **Target:** 1.2-1.6 g/kg/day
- **Higher end (1.4-1.6):** During heavy training blocks, caloric deficit, or combined endurance + strength training
- **Lower end (1.2-1.4):** Maintenance periods, lighter training

### During Caloric Restriction (Weight Loss Phases)
- **Target:** 1.6-2.0 g/kg/day
- Higher protein preserves lean mass during deficit
- Critical: don't cut protein when cutting calories

### Examples by Body Weight
| Weight | Low (1.2g/kg) | Moderate (1.4g/kg) | High (1.6g/kg) |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|----------------|
| 65 kg | 78g | 91g | 104g |
| 75 kg | 90g | 105g | 120g |
| 85 kg | 102g | 119g | 136g |

## Protein Timing and Distribution

### Optimal Distribution
- **4-5 doses per day** of 0.25-0.4 g/kg each (~20-30g per dose for a 75kg rider)
- Distribute evenly: breakfast, mid-morning, lunch, post-training, dinner
- Avoid loading all protein into one meal — muscle protein synthesis is stimulated per dose, not cumulative

### Post-Exercise Window
- Consume 20-30g protein within 60 minutes after training
- Combine with carbohydrates for optimal glycogen resynthesis
- Ideal ratio: ~3:1 carbs to protein for recovery meals
- Example: 60g carbs + 20g protein = rice with chicken, or recovery shake with banana

### Pre-Sleep Protein
- 30-40g casein or slow-digesting protein before bed can support overnight muscle repair
- Practical: Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, or casein shake

## Protein Sources — Quality Matters

### High-Quality Sources (complete amino acid profile)
- Eggs, chicken, fish, lean beef, dairy, whey protein
- Plant-based: soy, quinoa. Combine legumes + grains for complete profile.

### Leucine Content
Leucine is the primary amino acid triggering muscle protein synthesis. Each protein dose should contain at least 2-3g leucine.
- Whey protein: ~2.5g leucine per 25g serving (highest)
- Eggs: ~1.5g per 3 eggs
- Chicken breast: ~2.5g per 100g

## Practical Daily Example (75kg rider, moderate training, 1.4g/kg = 105g protein)

| Meal | Protein Source | Protein (g) |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Breakfast | 3 eggs + toast | 20g |
| Mid-morning | Greek yogurt + nuts | 20g |
| Lunch | Chicken breast + rice | 30g |
| Post-ride | Whey shake + banana | 25g |
| Dinner | Fish + vegetables | 25g |
| **Total** | | **~120g** |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Skipping post-ride protein:** The recovery window matters. Delaying protein delays repair.
2. **All protein at dinner:** Spreading intake across meals is significantly more effective for muscle protein synthesis.
3. **Cutting protein when cutting calories:** Protein should increase, not decrease, during weight loss phases.
4. **Ignoring protein on rest days:** Recovery happens on rest days. Protein intake should remain consistent.
