# In-Season Maintenance and the Minimal Effective Dose

## Overview

Almost every cyclist who starts strength training in the off-season abandons it by April. The reasoning is understandable — riding volume climbs, races start, and the gym feels like the obvious thing to cut. The consequence is that the strength built over the winter is largely lost by mid-season, exactly when it would have been useful.

The good news, and the point of this document: **maintaining strength requires far less work than building it.**

## What the Evidence Says About Maintenance

Spiering et al. (2021) reviewed the minimal dose of exercise needed to preserve endurance and strength over time. The consistent finding across the literature:

- **Training frequency and volume can be reduced substantially — often to as little as one session per week, or roughly one-third of the volume — without loss of strength**, provided intensity is maintained.
- **Intensity is the non-negotiable variable.** Load must stay high. A rider who keeps three weekly sessions but drops to light weights will lose strength; a rider who drops to one weekly session at heavy load will not.

This mirrors the tapering literature in endurance training almost exactly: cut volume, hold intensity.

Rønnestad et al. (2010) demonstrated the applied version in cyclists — in-season strength maintenance preserved the strength and performance gains made in the preparatory period, whereas ceasing strength training lost them.

## The In-Season Prescription

### Frequency
**One session per week is sufficient for maintenance.** Two is comfortable and provides a buffer for the weeks when one gets missed. Zero loses the adaptation over a matter of weeks to months.

### Volume
Roughly **one-third to one-half of build-phase volume**:
- 2-3 exercises instead of 5-6
- 2-3 sets per exercise instead of 3-4
- Compound lifts only — drop the accessory work first

### Intensity
**Maintain heavy loading: 3-6 reps at 80-90% of 1RM.** This is the variable that must not slip.

Leave 2-3 repetitions in reserve. In-season is not the time to grind to failure — the cost in fatigue and soreness outweighs any additional adaptation.

### Exercise Selection
Keep only what earns its place:
- **One bilateral lower-body compound** — squat or leg press
- **One hip-hinge pattern** — deadlift, Romanian deadlift, or hip thrust
- **One single-leg movement** — split squat or step-up (optional but valuable for asymmetry)
- **Core work** — brief, and only if the rider will actually do it

Drop: upper-body isolation, machine accessories, high-repetition burnout sets, anything novel.

### Duration
**A maintenance session should take 30-40 minutes including warm-up.** If it takes longer, it is not a maintenance session and will get skipped.

## A Sample In-Season Maintenance Session

**Warm-up (8 min):** 5 min easy spin or rower, bodyweight squats, hip openers, glute activation

**Main work:**
- Back squat — 3 × 4 at ~85% 1RM, 3 min rest
- Romanian deadlift — 2 × 5 at ~80% 1RM, 2 min rest
- Bulgarian split squat — 2 × 6 per leg, moderate load
- Plank variation — 2 × 30-45 s

**Total time: ~35 minutes.**

## Placement in the Week

The scheduling rules from the concurrent training and session timing documents apply with extra force in-season, because there is less room for error:

- **After a hard ride on the same day**, ideally separated by several hours — concentrates fatigue into one day.
- **Or on a genuine easy/recovery day**, accepting that the day is no longer fully easy.
- **Never the day before a key session or race.**
- **Never within 48 hours of a race.**

**Race week:** one light session early in the week (Monday or Tuesday for a weekend race), reduced load, or skip entirely. Skipping one week does not lose the adaptation.

## Deloading and Detraining

### When to Deload the Gym
Every 4-6 weeks during heavy blocks, cut load by ~20% for one week. This is less critical in maintenance mode than in build mode, since maintenance volume is already low.

### What Happens When You Stop
- **Neural adaptations** — the majority of early strength gain — decay relatively quickly, over a few weeks.
- **Muscle mass** decays more slowly.
- **Bone adaptations** decay slowly but are the hardest to rebuild (see the bone health document).
- **After a break of several weeks, do not resume at previous loads.** Re-enter at roughly 70-80% of previous working weights and rebuild over 2-3 weeks. Returning straight to old numbers is the most common way riders injure themselves and confirm their belief that the gym is not worth it.

### Planned Breaks Are Fine
A rider taking two weeks off around a major target event will not undo their winter. The problem is never a planned two-week break — it is the unplanned break that quietly becomes six months.

## Practical Adherence — The Actual Bottleneck

The programme that gets done beats the optimal programme that does not. When a rider is struggling to maintain:

- **Cut to one session, fixed day, fixed time.** Consistency of scheduling beats optimality of content.
- **Shorten it.** A 25-minute session that happens is worth more than a 60-minute session that does not.
- **Attach it to an existing habit** — immediately after a specific weekly ride, at the same gym, same day.
- **Home options count.** With limited equipment, single-leg work, hip thrusts, and loaded carries maintain far more than nothing. The bone-loading benefit is reduced without heavy axial loading, but the muscular maintenance largely holds.
- **Set the expectation up front** — in the off-season, tell the rider the plan drops to one weekly session in season. Riders who expect to maintain 3 sessions year-round quit when they cannot.

## Annual Structure Summary

| Phase | Frequency | Volume | Intensity | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Off-season / anatomical adaptation | 2-3 ×/week | High, higher reps | Moderate | Tissue preparation, technique |
| Off-season / max strength | 2-3 ×/week | Moderate | Heavy (3-6 reps, 80-90%) | Build strength |
| Base / early build | 2 ×/week | Reduced | Heavy | Continue building, transition |
| Build / in-season | 1-2 ×/week | Low | Heavy | **Maintain** |
| Race week | 0-1 ×/week | Minimal | Moderate | Preserve without fatigue |
| Transition / post-season | 0-1 ×/week | Minimal | Light | Rest, then restart |
