# Power Zones Explained — Coggan 7-Zone Model

## Overview

Power zones define intensity ranges relative to an athlete's Functional Threshold Power (FTP). Every workout prescription, performance analysis, and training load calculation is built on this framework. The Coggan Classic 7-zone model is the standard in power-based cycling training.

## What Is FTP?

Functional Threshold Power (FTP) is the highest power output a rider can sustain in a quasi-steady state for approximately one hour. When power exceeds FTP, fatigue accumulates rapidly. When power is below FTP, the effort can be sustained considerably longer.

FTP serves as the anchor for all training zones — every zone is defined as a percentage of FTP. Reassess FTP every 6-8 weeks, or after significant fitness changes.

## The 7 Power Zones

### Zone 1 — Active Recovery
- **Power:** <55% of FTP
- **RPE:** 1-2 | **Duration:** 30-90 min
- **Purpose:** Recovery blood flow, no training stress. Easy spinning, no force on pedals.
- **When to use:** Recovery days, warmup, cooldown.

### Zone 2 — Endurance
- **Power:** 56-75% of FTP
- **RPE:** 2-3 | **Duration:** 1-5+ hours
- **Purpose:** Aerobic base, fat oxidation, mitochondrial density. The foundation of training volume.
- **When to use:** Long rides, base training, majority of weekly hours.
- **Feel:** Comfortable. Full-sentence conversation. Controlled breathing.

### Zone 3 — Tempo
- **Power:** 76-90% of FTP
- **RPE:** 3-4 | **Duration:** 30 min - 3 hours
- **Purpose:** Muscular endurance, sustained power.
- **Feel:** Requires concentration. Short-phrase conversation. "Comfortably hard."
- **Caution:** In polarized training, Zone 3 is "no man's land" — too hard to recover from quickly, too easy for top-end adaptations. Use purposefully.

### Zone 4 — Lactate Threshold
- **Power:** 91-105% of FTP
- **RPE:** 4-5 | **Duration:** 10-60 min
- **Purpose:** Raises FTP directly. Improves power at lactate accumulation point.
- **Feel:** Hard. Heavy breathing. Single-word conversation only.
- **When to use:** Threshold intervals (2x20min, 3x15min), time trials.

### Zone 5 — VO2max
- **Power:** 106-120% of FTP
- **RPE:** 6-7 | **Duration:** 3-8 min per interval
- **Purpose:** Increases maximal oxygen uptake. Cardiovascular ceiling improvement.
- **Feel:** Very hard. Labored breathing. Legs burn. Cannot sustain beyond minutes.
- **When to use:** VO2max intervals (4x4min, 5x5min). Build and peak phases.

### Zone 6 — Anaerobic Capacity
- **Power:** 121-150% of FTP
- **RPE:** 7-9 | **Duration:** 30s - 3 min per interval
- **Purpose:** Anaerobic energy system. Sustain efforts above VO2max. Attacks, short climbs, surges.
- **When to use:** Short intervals (30s-2min on, equal or greater rest). Race-specific prep.

### Zone 7 — Neuromuscular Power
- **Power:** >150% of FTP
- **RPE:** 10 | **Duration:** <30 seconds
- **Purpose:** Peak power, sprint ability, maximal muscle fiber recruitment.
- **When to use:** Sprint training, race finishes, jump intervals. Full recovery between efforts.

## Sweet Spot — The Critical Sub-Zone

Sweet spot targets 88-94% of FTP — upper Zone 3 / lower Zone 4. Not an official Coggan zone but universally used.

- **Power:** 88-94% of FTP | **RPE:** 4 | **Duration:** 10-30 min intervals
- **Purpose:** Maximum training stimulus per unit of fatigue. Approximately 95% of threshold adaptation at roughly 50% of the fatigue cost.
- **Best for:** Time-crunched athletes (<8 hours/week). High return on limited training time.
- **Example:** FTP 250W → Sweet Spot is 220-235W.

## Heart Rate vs Power

Heart rate and power zones do not map 1:1. Heart rate lags power changes by 30-60 seconds, drifts upward during sustained effort, and is affected by heat, hydration, caffeine, fatigue, and altitude. Use power as primary for intervals. Heart rate is useful as secondary — especially for Zone 2 rides to ensure truly easy effort.

## Indoor vs Outdoor

Indoor trainer feels harder at the same wattage: no coasting, less cooling, constant pedaling. Adjust indoor targets down 5-10% from outdoor FTP, or test separately. Many athletes maintain two FTP values.
