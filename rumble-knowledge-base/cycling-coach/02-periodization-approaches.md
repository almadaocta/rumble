# Periodization Approaches for Cycling

## Overview

Periodization is the systematic structuring of training into phases over weeks or months, designed to reach peak fitness at the right time. Different approaches suit different athletes depending on available hours, experience, goals, and time to target event.

## Classical (Linear) Periodization

### What It Is
Progressive phases moving from general to specific fitness across a season. Based on Matveyev's model, adapted for cycling by Friel.

### Phases
1. **Preparation / Base (8-12 weeks):** High volume, low intensity. Build aerobic foundation. Zone 1-2 focus. Gym strength work begins.
2. **Build (6-8 weeks):** Volume decreases slightly, intensity increases. Sweet spot and threshold work introduced. Race-specific intervals.
3. **Peak (2-4 weeks):** Reduced volume, high intensity. Race simulation. VO2max and race-pace efforts.
4. **Taper (1-2 weeks):** Volume drops 40-60%. Intensity maintained but fewer intervals. Goal: arrive fresh with fitness intact.
5. **Race:** Target event(s). TSB positive (+10 to +25).
6. **Transition / Recovery (2-4 weeks):** Active recovery. No structure. Ride for fun. Mental and physical reset.

### Best For
Athletes with one clear target event and 16+ weeks to prepare. Works well for gran fondos, key road races, cycling tours.

### Not Ideal For
Athletes racing frequently throughout the season. Athletes with very limited time (<6h/week) who need intensity year-round.

## Polarized Training (80/20)

### What It Is
Training distribution where approximately 80% of training time is spent at low intensity (Zone 1-2) and approximately 20% at high intensity (Zone 4-5+), with minimal time in Zone 3 (Tempo).

### Key Principles
- Zone 3 is deliberately avoided — it creates fatigue without the same adaptation stimulus as higher intensity
- Low-intensity sessions must be truly easy (below lactate threshold 1)
- High-intensity sessions must be truly hard (above lactate threshold 2)
- Typically 2 high-intensity sessions per week is sufficient
- The remaining sessions are Zone 1-2

### Evidence
Seiler (2010) established that elite endurance athletes across sports naturally gravitate toward ~80/20 distribution. Stöggl & Sperlich (2015) showed polarized produced superior VO2max improvements vs threshold or high-volume-low-intensity in well-trained athletes. Muñoz et al. (2014) demonstrated polarized benefits extend to recreational athletes.

### Best For
Athletes with 10+ hours/week. Strong aerobic base already established. Experienced athletes who can execute truly easy and truly hard sessions.

### Not Ideal For
Time-crunched athletes (<6h/week) — insufficient volume to make 80% low-intensity meaningful. Beginners who need variety and progressive overload.

## Sweet Spot Training

### What It Is
A training approach centered on the sweet spot intensity (88-94% of FTP). High training stimulus relative to fatigue cost.

### Key Principles
- 3-4 sweet spot sessions per week
- Intervals of 10-30 minutes
- Builds FTP effectively with less recovery demand than pure threshold work
- Can be combined with endurance rides and occasional VO2max work

### Best For
Time-crunched athletes with 5-8 hours/week. Intermediate riders building FTP. Athletes who respond well to moderate-high steady-state efforts.

### Not Ideal For
High-volume athletes who can afford true polarized approach. Race week (too much fatigue). Athletes at risk of overtraining (sweet spot still accumulates significant TSS).

## Block Periodization

### What It Is
Concentrated loading blocks of 2-4 weeks focusing on a single ability, followed by recovery. Based on Issurin's model.

### Structure Example
- Block 1 (3 weeks): Aerobic endurance focus → 1 week recovery
- Block 2 (3 weeks): Threshold focus → 1 week recovery
- Block 3 (2 weeks): VO2max focus → 1 week recovery → race

### Best For
Competitive athletes. Short focused build phases. Athletes who respond well to concentrated stimulus followed by recovery.

### Not Ideal For
Beginners who need variety. Athletes who can't handle concentrated training loads.

## Pyramidal Distribution

### What It Is
Most time in Zone 1-2, moderate time in Zone 3, less in Zone 4-5. Differs from polarized by including more Zone 3 (threshold/tempo) work.

### Distribution
Zone 1-2: 70-80% | Zone 3: 15-20% | Zone 4-5: 5-10%

### Best For
Moderate volume (8-12 hours/week). Year-round consistency. Athletes who tolerate tempo well.

### Not Ideal For
Specific peaking for an event (polarized or block may be more effective for that).

## How to Choose

| Factor | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|
| <6h/week available | Sweet spot primary. Consider polarized with fewer but harder sessions. |
| 6-10h/week | Pyramidal or sweet spot. Both work well. |
| 10+ hours/week | Polarized becomes viable and often superior. |
| Beginner | Variety and progressive overload. No strict distribution needed. |
| Target event in 16+ weeks | Classical periodization. Base → Build → Peak → Taper. |
| Racing frequently | Reverse periodization or block approach. |
| Off-season | Higher volume, lower intensity. Build aerobic base. |
| In-season | Maintain fitness with reduced volume, race-specific intensity. |

The athlete's history and response to training should override these defaults. Track what works — some time-crunched athletes respond better to polarized with fewer but harder sessions than to sweet spot.
