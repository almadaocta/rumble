# FTP Testing Protocols

## Overview

FTP (Functional Threshold Power) is the foundation of all zone calculations. Testing must be standardized for reliable comparison over time. Three primary protocols exist, each with trade-offs.

## General Testing Rules

1. **Consistency:** Same course (or indoor trainer), same time of day, similar conditions.
2. **Freshness:** Test after a rest day or easy day. No hard sessions in prior 48 hours.
3. **Fueling:** Eat normally. Don't test fasted or overfueled.
4. **Warmup:** 15-20 min progressive warmup including 2-3 short hard efforts to prime the legs.
5. **Pacing:** Start conservatively. The most common mistake is going too hard in the first minutes.
6. **Frequency:** Retest every 6-8 weeks, or after significant training blocks.

## Protocol 1: 20-Minute Test

### How It Works
Ride as hard as possible for 20 minutes. Take 95% of average power as estimated FTP.

### Structure
1. Warmup: 20 min progressive (Zone 1 → Zone 3)
2. 3x 1-min hard efforts at ~Zone 5 with 1 min recovery (prime the legs)
3. 5 min easy spinning
4. **20 minutes: all-out time trial effort**
5. Cooldown: 10-15 min easy

### Calculating FTP
FTP = Average Power for 20 min × 0.95

Example: 20-min average = 280W → FTP = 280 × 0.95 = 266W

### Why 95%?
The 5% reduction accounts for the difference between 20-minute and 60-minute sustainable power. Most athletes can hold 20-min power approximately 5% above their true threshold.

### Strengths
- Simple, widely understood
- Can be done indoor or outdoor
- Good for tracking progress over time

### Weaknesses
- Requires strong pacing — going too hard early ruins the result
- The 95% multiplier is an approximation — some athletes may be 92-97% depending on their physiology (time trialists vs punchy riders)
- Psychologically demanding — requires true all-out effort

## Protocol 2: Ramp Test

### How It Works
Progressive increase in power until failure. FTP estimated as 75% of the last completed minute's power.

### Structure
1. Start at ~100W (or 50% of expected FTP)
2. Increase by 20W every minute (or 5-6% every minute)
3. Continue until you cannot maintain the required power
4. Record the power of the last fully completed minute

### Calculating FTP
FTP = Last Completed Minute Power × 0.75

Example: Failed at 360W step, last completed = 340W → FTP = 340 × 0.75 = 255W

### Strengths
- Short duration (~15-20 min of progressive effort)
- No pacing required — just hold on as long as possible
- Easy to execute, especially indoors
- Repeatable and objective

### Weaknesses
- Tends to favor athletes with high anaerobic capacity (can overestimate FTP for punchy riders)
- May underestimate FTP for steady-state athletes (time trialists)
- Less accurate than 20-min test for athletes with unusual power profiles

## Protocol 3: 2x8-Minute Test

### How It Works
Two 8-minute all-out efforts with 10 minutes recovery. Average the power of both efforts and take 90% as estimated FTP.

### Structure
1. Warmup: 15 min progressive
2. 3x 1-min hard efforts to prime legs
3. 5 min easy
4. **8 minutes: all-out**
5. 10 min easy recovery
6. **8 minutes: all-out**
7. Cooldown: 10 min

### Calculating FTP
FTP = Average of both 8-min powers × 0.90

Example: Effort 1 = 300W, Effort 2 = 290W → Average = 295W → FTP = 295 × 0.90 = 266W

### Strengths
- Shorter hard efforts are psychologically easier than 20 minutes
- Two efforts improve reliability (bad pacing on one is buffered)
- Good for athletes who struggle with long sustained efforts

### Weaknesses
- Less commonly used — harder to compare with others
- The 90% multiplier is approximate
- Two hard efforts in one session adds complexity

## Which Test to Use?

| Athlete Type | Recommended Test | Why |
|-------------|-----------------|-----|
| Time trialist / steady-state | 20-minute test | Best approximation of their strength |
| Punchy / anaerobic | 20-minute test | Ramp test may overestimate due to high anaerobic capacity |
| New to testing | Ramp test | Simple, no pacing skill needed |
| Psychologically dislikes long efforts | 2x8-minute test | Shorter hard efforts |
| Indoor only | Ramp test or 20-min | Both work well on trainer |
| Wants most accurate result | 20-minute test on a known course | Most validated protocol |

## When NOT to Update FTP

- **One exceptional ride:** Don't update FTP from a single high 20-min power. Verify repeatability first.
- **Downhill or tailwind 20-min effort:** Overestimates true threshold.
- **Race data only:** Race adrenaline can push power above sustainable training threshold.
- **More than 10% jump:** If new FTP is >10% higher than previous, verify before updating all zones. Train at the new zones for a week and confirm RPE alignment.

The coach should confirm FTP updates. It is better to undertrain by 10% with conservative zones than to overtrain by 1% with aggressive zones.

## Tracking FTP Over Time

FTP changes should be logged with date, test type, and context. A history allows:
- Tracking fitness progression across training blocks
- Identifying seasonal peaks and valleys
- Correlating FTP changes with training load (CTL)

Typical FTP improvement rates:
- Beginner (first year): 15-30% improvement possible
- Intermediate (2-4 years): 5-10% per year
- Advanced (5+ years): 1-3% per year (diminishing returns)
