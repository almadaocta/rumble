# TSB Interpretation Guide — Managing Form and Freshness

## Overview

Training Stress Balance (TSB) is a key metric for managing the balance between fitness and fatigue. TSB = CTL - ATL (Chronic Training Load minus Acute Training Load). It represents an athlete's "form" — their readiness to perform on any given day.

## Understanding the Components

### CTL — Chronic Training Load (Fitness)
- Rolling 42-day exponential moving average of daily TSS
- Represents accumulated fitness over approximately 6 weeks
- Higher CTL = more fit (but also potentially more fatigued)
- Changes slowly — takes consistent training over weeks to shift meaningfully
- Typical ranges: 40-60 (recreational), 60-90 (serious amateur), 90-130+ (competitive/elite)

### ATL — Acute Training Load (Fatigue)
- Rolling 7-day exponential moving average of daily TSS
- Represents recent training stress over approximately 1 week
- Spikes when you train hard, drops when you rest
- Changes quickly — responds to each individual training day

### TSB — Training Stress Balance (Form)
- TSB = CTL - ATL
- When CTL > ATL (positive TSB): you are fresher than your fitness level — ready to perform
- When ATL > CTL (negative TSB): you are carrying more fatigue than usual — building fitness but not ready to race
- TSB is a relative measure — it tells you about your current state relative to YOUR baseline

## TSB Ranges and What They Mean

| TSB Range | State | Practical Meaning |
|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| +15 to +30 | Very fresh | Peak performance window. Ideal for A-priority races. Extended freshness suggests possible detraining if maintained too long. |
| +5 to +15 | Fresh | Good for key sessions, B-races, hard group rides. Can push performance. |
| -5 to +5 | Neutral | Normal productive training. Body is absorbing training load appropriately. |
| -5 to -15 | Moderate fatigue | Typical during build phases. Performance slightly suppressed. Continue if subjective feel is OK. |
| -15 to -25 | Significant fatigue | Approaching limits. Scale back intensity. Watch for overreaching signs. |
| Below -25 | High fatigue / Overreaching | Immediate load reduction. Risk of NFOR. No new intensity. |

## Critical Nuance: TSB Is Not Everything

TSB is a model, not a measurement. It must be cross-referenced with subjective markers:

- **TSB -10, athlete feels great:** Continue training. The model says moderate fatigue, but the body says fine. Trust the body.
- **TSB +5, athlete feels terrible:** Something is wrong — poor sleep, illness, life stress, nutritional deficit. The math says fresh, but the body disagrees. Rest.
- **TSB -20, planned and expected:** This is normal during a build block. Watch for warning signs but don't panic about the number alone.

## Managing TSB for Key Events

### Taper Protocol
To arrive fresh for a target event:
1. **3-4 weeks out:** TSB will be negative (-10 to -20) from build training. Normal.
2. **2-3 weeks out:** Begin reducing volume. Keep some intensity. TSB starts rising.
3. **1 week out:** Significant volume reduction. 1-2 short sharp sessions. TSB approaches 0.
4. **2-3 days out:** Light spinning or rest. TSB rises to +5 to +15.
5. **Race day target TSB:** +10 to +25.

### Common Taper Mistakes
- **Too much rest:** TSB goes above +25. Athlete feels flat, legs feel "dead." Some stimulus is needed.
- **Too little taper:** TSB still negative on race day. Athlete is fit but fatigued.
- **Intensity removed:** Volume should drop, but 1-2 short intensity sessions maintain race sharpness.
- **Taper too long:** More than 2 weeks of significant volume reduction can reduce CTL meaningfully. Fitness is perishable.

## CTL Ramp Rate — Building Fitness Safely

The rate at which CTL increases matters:

| Ramp Rate (CTL points/week) | Risk Level | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|------------|----------------|
| 3-5 | Low | Safe for most athletes. Sustainable long-term progression. |
| 5-7 | Moderate | Acceptable for limited periods (4-6 weeks). Monitor recovery. |
| 7-10 | High | Significant overreaching risk. Only with careful monitoring. |
| >10 | Very high | Almost certainly leads to NFOR or illness. Avoid. |

**Rule of thumb:** If CTL is rising faster than 7 points/week for more than 2 consecutive weeks, reduce training load.

## Deload Weeks and TSB

Every 3-4 weeks, a deload week should bring TSB back to neutral or slightly positive:
- Reduce weekly TSS by 40-50%
- Zone 1-2 only
- TSB should rise from negative to approximately 0 to +10
- This "resets" the system and allows adaptation from the previous training block

After the deload, the next build block starts with the athlete fresh and ready to absorb new training stress.

## Using TSB for Daily Decisions

### Morning Check-In
Each morning, the coach should check:
1. **TSB number:** Where is the athlete on the form spectrum?
2. **TSB trend:** Rising (recovering), falling (accumulating fatigue), or stable?
3. **Subjective feel:** How does the athlete actually feel?

### Decision Framework
- TSB falling + athlete feels OK = continue as planned
- TSB falling + athlete reports fatigue = consider easy day or rest
- TSB stable + athlete feels great = may be time to increase training stimulus
- TSB rising + athlete feels sluggish = may need one more day of recovery before intensity
