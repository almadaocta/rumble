# Recovery Timelines by Session Type

## Overview

Recovery is not a uniform process. Different session types create different levels of physiological stress and require different recovery durations before the athlete can perform quality training again. These timelines guide when to schedule the next hard session.

## Recovery by Session Type

### Zone 1 — Active Recovery Ride (30-60 min)
- **Recovery needed:** 0 hours (this IS recovery)
- **Status next day:** Fully fresh
- **Notes:** Can be done daily. Should feel easier than doing nothing.

### Zone 2 — Endurance Ride (2-3 hours)
- **Recovery needed:** 12-24 hours
- **Status next day:** Ready for moderate or hard session
- **Notes:** Low-stress session. If nutrition and sleep are good, back-to-back Zone 2 days are fine.

### Zone 2 — Long Endurance Ride (4-5+ hours)
- **Recovery needed:** 24-48 hours
- **Status next day:** Light activity only
- **Notes:** Glycogen depletion is the primary limiter. Aggressive post-ride nutrition (1.0-1.2 g/kg carbs within 60 min) accelerates recovery.

### Sweet Spot Intervals (2x20 or 3x12 min at 88-94% FTP)
- **Recovery needed:** 24-36 hours
- **Status next day:** Easy ride OK. Next hard session in 36-48 hours.
- **Notes:** Moderate muscular and metabolic stress. Key to recovery is carbohydrate replenishment.

### Zone 4 — Threshold Intervals (2x20 min at 95-105% FTP)
- **Recovery needed:** 36-48 hours
- **Status next day:** Active recovery or rest only
- **Notes:** Significant metabolic stress. Lactate threshold work depletes glycogen and creates systemic fatigue.

### Zone 5 — VO2max Intervals (5x5 min at 106-120% FTP)
- **Recovery needed:** 36-48 hours
- **Status next day:** Active recovery or rest only
- **Notes:** High cardiovascular and muscular stress. Neural fatigue component. Sleep quality critical for recovery.

### Zone 6 — Anaerobic Capacity Intervals (8x1 min at 135-150% FTP)
- **Recovery needed:** 24-36 hours
- **Status next day:** Easy ride OK
- **Notes:** High neuromuscular stress but short total work time. Less glycogen depletion than threshold. Neural recovery takes priority.

### Gym — Max Strength Session
- **Recovery needed:** 48-72 hours (cycling-specific output)
- **Status next day:** Easy spin only. DOMS expected at 24-48h.
- **Notes:** Muscular damage peaks at 24-48h. Quality cycling is compromised for 48-72h after heavy legs. Decreases with training adaptation (4-6 weeks).

### Race (Road Race, Criterium, Gran Fondo)
- **Recovery needed:** 48-72+ hours
- **Status next day:** Active recovery or complete rest
- **Notes:** Racing combines physical stress with psychological and nervous system stress. Full recovery takes longer than an equivalent training session.

## Factors That Modify Recovery

### Faster Recovery
- Good sleep (7-9 hours, quality deep sleep)
- Adequate post-ride nutrition (carbs + protein within 60 min)
- Low life stress
- Good aerobic fitness (higher CTL = faster recovery)
- Younger age
- Adequate hydration

### Slower Recovery
- Poor or short sleep (<6 hours)
- Missed post-ride nutrition
- High life/work stress
- Low fitness level (low CTL)
- Illness (even mild)
- Travel / jet lag
- Alcohol consumption
- Heat stress

## Practical Weekly Recovery Patterns

### Pattern A: Hard-Easy-Hard
Mon: Rest → Tue: Hard → Wed: Easy → Thu: Hard → Fri: Easy/Rest → Sat: Long → Sun: Easy

### Pattern B: Hard Block + Recovery
Mon: Hard → Tue: Hard → Wed: Rest → Thu: Easy → Fri: Hard → Sat: Long → Sun: Easy

Pattern A is safer for most athletes. Pattern B creates functional overreaching and requires careful monitoring — use only with experienced athletes who recover well.

## When to Take an Unplanned Rest Day

Take a complete rest day (override the plan) if:
- RPE for easy efforts is 2+ points higher than normal
- Resting heart rate is elevated by >5 bpm
- Sleep quality has been poor for 2+ consecutive nights
- Persistent muscle soreness that doesn't resolve with warmup
- Low motivation combined with physical fatigue symptoms
- Any sign of illness (sore throat, sniffles, unusual fatigue)

One unplanned rest day is better than three forced rest days from overtraining.
